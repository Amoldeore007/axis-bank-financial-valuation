## 📌 Executive Summary
Between FY2020 and FY2025, Axis Bank completed one of the sharpest operational turnarounds in Indian private banking:
- **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Surged from ₹43.54 Cr (FY20) to ₹22,182 Cr (FY25), driven by credit normalization and fee income growth.
- **Revenue Growth:** Expanded at a **14.2% CAGR** to ₹1,52,145 Cr.
- **Asset Base:** Crossed **₹16.11 Lakh Crore** (+12% CAGR).
- **Balance Sheet Strengthening:** Debt-to-Equity reduced from **1.74x to 1.02x**, fueled by an **111.6% organic increase in equity** through retained earnings.

## 📊 Core Financial Methodologies & Results

| Module | Methodology / Assumption | Key Output | Takeaway |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Time Value of Money (DDM)** | Gordon Growth Model ($Ke=12\%$, $g=0\%$) | Intrinsic Value: **₹8.33** | DDM is structurally unsuitable for growth banks retaining ~99% of earnings; multiples ($P/B$, $P/E$) are more reliable. |
| **Cost of Capital (WACC)** | $Ke=12\%$, Adjusted $Kd=7\%$, Tax Rate $=18\%$ | **WACC ≈ 9%** | Reflects low-cost funding advantage and serves as the hurdle rate for investment appraisals. |
| **Capital Budgeting** | Hypothetical ₹10,000 Cr Capex / Branch Expansion | **NPV: ₹3,343.77 Cr**<br>**IRR: 20%**<br>**Payback: 3.25 Yrs** | Financially viable and highly accretive (IRR is 2.22x higher than WACC). |
| **Leverage Analysis** | Operating & Financial Leverage ($DOL$, $DFL$) | **Peak DOL: 3.29x (FY24)**<br>**DFL: Negative** | Negative DFL and sub-1x interest coverage are banking sector norms due to deposit interest accounting. |
| **Capital Allocation** | Payout Ratio vs Retention Ratio | **Retention: 99%**<br>**DPS: ₹1.00** | Sustained internal accrual policy to maintain RBI Basel III Tier-1 capital adequacy. |
